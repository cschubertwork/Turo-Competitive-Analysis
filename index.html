Turo x New Zealand Competitive analysis

Two collapses say more about a New Zealand launch than five competitors do

Framing lens: New Zealand market entry, competitive tension, cultural fit, and sports partnerships · Generated 2026-09-22

What this found

Fleet-owned car sharing keeps failing the same way

Mevo, reported as Wellington's car-share leader, collapsed into voluntary administration in March 2026. Getaround, Turo's closest global peer, is mid-liquidation the same year. One is a small local operator, the other an international one, and they ran into the same wall: this business runs on thin margin, and it breaks the moment a funding round slips. Turo never carries that exposure in the first place, since it doesn't own a fleet.

This field has fewer owners than the five names suggest

Mevo and Zilch are both now owned by Carbn Group. Cityhop sits inside Toyota New Zealand. Turo would really be negotiating against two corporate owners in the direct car-share category, and everything each of them controls, from parking deals to insurance terms, comes down to a decision made somewhere inside Carbn or Toyota.

Nobody sponsors sport here

Not one competitor profiled, car-share or traditional rental, uses sport or events for brand-building. Every signal found runs through motoring bodies, energy companies, sustainability awards, or a parent's own finance arm. Whoever moves first on this gets it uncontested.

The trust bar is set locally

GO Rentals, an NZ-owned rental company, is positioned ahead of international agency brands on customer satisfaction. A new entrant's credibility problem is proving itself trustworthy inside New Zealand; the international agencies are already losing that fight on their own.

None of these five sell the trip Turo is built for

Every NZ-specific competitor here is built for short, local, back-to-base or free-floating trips, or for agency-counter travel rental. None of them serve Turo's real case elsewhere: multi-day trips across a wide variety of individually owned vehicles. The lane is open. Building host supply from zero to fill it is the hard part.

Cluster

Corporately-backed incumbents

Cityhop · Zilch · Mevo (post-relaunch)

All three NZ-specific car-share brands now sit inside a larger parent, Toyota NZ or Carbn Group, rather than standing alone on venture or crowdfunded capital. Their brand-building runs through infrastructure and finance partnerships instead: Toyota Financial Services, the AA, an energy company's own marketing budget.

For Turo: there's no scrappy-independent position left to claim here, since every current player already has a well-capitalised parent behind it. What Turo would be selling is the marketplace model itself, and that has to carry the whole pitch.
Cluster

The cautionary tale

Pre-relaunch Mevo · Getaround (global)

Both were fleet-financed or fleet-dependent car-share operators that grew fast, raised real capital, and still ran out of runway.

For Turo: Mevo and Getaround prove the same point from opposite sides of the world. Owning or financing a fleet is where car-sharing companies die. Turo skipped that risk by design, and an NZ launch pitch should open with it, not bury it in a slide about vehicle variety.
Cluster

Traditional rental incumbent

GO Rentals

Owned fleet, agency counters, trust built on tourism-industry awards and a global brand licence (Enterprise, National, Alamo) rather than community partnerships.

For Turo: it competes for the same traveler dollar, just on a different axis: guaranteed availability and simplicity against selection and price. Think of GO Rentals less as a product rival and more as the trust bar a new entrant has to clear.
Outlier

Turo itself

No current NZ presence

The only asset-light marketplace in the set, and the only participant with no operating history in this market. Every comparison here is against a hypothetical entry.

Reading it: every strength listed below is a claim about how the model should work here, since there's no New Zealand track record yet to back it up.

Turo's position against this field

Strengths

+Zero fleet-financing exposure, the exact failure mode that broke Mevo and Getaround
+Proven variable, demand-based commission (March 2026 US pilots) that could tune for a small market
+Far broader vehicle variety and trip-length flexibility than any NZ car-share operator offers

Gaps

−Missing the NZ insurance, ACC, parking, and council relationships every profiled competitor already has in some form
−Zero local host supply on day one, a cold-start problem none of the fleet-owning competitors face
−Nothing yet to signal trust, in a market where an NZ-owned incumbent already outperforms international brands on reputation

Recommendations

  1. Lead with the asset-light story first.Mevo's collapse and Getaround's dissolution are recent, sourceable, and locally legible. Use them directly: "no fleet to run out of money on" is a stronger opener than "more cars."
  2. Solve host supply before marketing to guests.Every incumbent here already owns or leases its fleet, so none of them has Turo's problem: finding people willing to list their own car before there's a single guest to book it. That's the real bottleneck this launch hits first.
  3. Sponsor tourism and events before rugby.Turo's real NZ customer looks like a traveler booking a car for a few days rather than a season-ticket holder. Queenstown ski season, marathon and cycling events, and wine-region road trips reach that buyer directly, at a fraction of national rugby's cost and clutter.
  4. Replicate the infrastructure deals before competing on them.Cityhop's Z Energy parking tie and Mevo's AA partnership are table stakes; a new entrant needs some version of each before it can compete on anything else.
  5. Target the multi-day trip.No NZ-specific competitor profiled serves multi-day, wide-selection travel rental well. That's the strength Turo already has everywhere else, and here, nobody's contesting it.

Founded 2007, Toyota NZ-owned since 2018. The most corporately stable of the three NZ car-share brands. No public figures more recent than 2020 found. No visible link to Toyota's own significant motorsport sponsorship portfolio (the GR86 Championship), despite sharing a parent.

Zilch (formerly Yoogo Share) Depth: full›

Launched Christchurch 2018, rebranded to Zilch, acquired by Carbn Group in December 2023 (later also acquiring Mevo out of administration). Widest footprint of any NZ-specific competitor at 5 cities, 17 hubs. Business-fleet and sustainability-award brand identity; subscription-leaning pricing rather than per-trip.

GO Rentals Depth: light›

NZ-owned traditional rental incumbent, licenced to run Enterprise, National, and Alamo in New Zealand since 2022. Positioned ahead of international agency brands on customer satisfaction in market coverage. No sponsorship activity found beyond tourism-industry awards.

Getaround Depth: light›

Turo's closest global peer-to-peer model. Exited the US in 2025, sold its European business to GoMore in April 2026, and its board approved dissolution and liquidation in June 2026. Never operated in New Zealand; relevant here purely as a global model comparison.

How this repo was built, and what it can't tell you

A worked example of an AI-run competitive research process

The process

01

Setup

Product positioning for Turo drafted from public sources; competitor list and comparison template proposed and approved.

02

Light pass

Every competitor researched at survey depth: a snapshot plus the sections core to this analysis's framing lens.

03

Deep dive

Mevo, Cityhop, and Zilch researched at full depth, taken together because they compete directly in the same local market and the light pass surfaced findings worth triangulating.

04

Citation check

Every Observed claim in the deep profiles re-fetched from its cited URL. Unsupported sources were downgraded to Inferred, visibly.

05

Analysis and dashboard

Cross-competitor analysis generated on the framing lens, then rendered into this page directly from the profiles.

This gets more valuable every time you run it

Nothing here is a one-time snapshot. The profiles, the competitor list, and the analysis are all meant to be revisited and built on as this market changes.

Profiles build on what's already there

/competitive-update re-researches a tracked competitor and diffs the new findings against what's already written, sorting each one into material, minor, or no change. Coming back to this in six months means reading a short, sorted list of what changed.

Depth gets added one competitor at a time

GO Rentals or Getaround can go through the same full-depth, citation-verified treatment Mevo, Cityhop, and Zilch got here, without re-running the light pass on the other four.

The analysis can't quietly go stale

The comparison and the dashboard always regenerate from whatever the profiles currently say, so there's no separate analysis file drifting out of sync while the underlying research moves on underneath it.

Every update leaves a record

Once a repo is published, each research pass becomes its own commit. Over a few cycles, the git history becomes its own timeline of what changed in this market and when it happened.

The labelling discipline

Every factual claim in every profile carries one of two labels. Unlabelled claims are treated as inferred by default.

- Observed: [claim] (https://source-url)
- Inferred (assumption): [claim]

Sourced claims are re-checked against the pages they cite, not verified true. A vendor's own marketing figure that its own page supports is still a vendor claim, and stays marked as one.

What counts as material

A finding that contradicts an existing Observed claim is material. A finding that contradicts an Inferred one is minor, since it was already flagged as uncertain. New information is classified by how much it changes the competitive picture; when uncertain, the process leans toward over-flagging rather than under-flagging.

Trust boundary

All content retrieved by web search or page fetch is treated as untrusted external data: facts get extracted from it, but instructions embedded in it are never followed. No such attempt was encountered while building this repo.

What this method cannot tell you

This is desk research from public sources, produced on 2026-09-22. There are no win/loss interviews, customer calls, or analyst briefings behind it. Published announcements are a lagging and partial view of what any company has running with customers, so nothing here should be read as an audit of a competitor's internal capability. Fleet sizes and membership counts for the NZ-specific competitors are, in most cases, the most recent figures any public source disclosed, not necessarily current.
