# New Zealand context

*New Zealand context facts used by the analysis and dashboard, outside the competitor profiles and their label count. Checked 2026-10-03.*

## Licensing and inspection

- Observed: New Zealand's Land Transport Act 1998 defines a "rental service" as the letting of a motor vehicle on hire for carrying passengers or goods to a person who drives it or supplies a driver, and a rental service licence is one of the transport service licences the Act requires. (https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1998/0110/latest/DLM433613.html)
- Observed: operating an unlicensed rental service in New Zealand can carry a fine of up to $10,000 for a first conviction and up to $25,000 for later ones, and the court may impound the vehicles used for up to 90 days regardless of who owns them. (https://web.archive.org/web/20230129213257/https://nzta.govt.nz/commercial-driving/transport-service-licences/rental-service/)
- Observed: a vehicle used as a rental needs a certificate of fitness instead of the warrant of fitness that applies to an ordinary private car. (https://www.govt.nz/browse/transport/keeping-a-vehicle-on-the-road/get-a-warrant-of-fitness-or-certificate-of-fitness/)
- Observed: the 2017 Land Transport Amendment Act's "facilitated cost-sharing arrangement" exemption covers only a carpooling arrangement where a passenger reimburses the driver's costs, not vehicle rental, so it gives a peer-to-peer car rental platform no exemption from the ordinary rental-service licensing regime. (https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1998/0110/latest/DLM433613.html)
- Inferred (assumption): NZTA's apparent position is that on a peer-to-peer platform, the platform itself, not the individual host, is the transport service operator who must hold the rental service licence; this is known only from a search-engine snippet, because the live NZTA rental-service page blocks automated access for every method tried, including the Wayback Machine's own re-crawl of it.

## Insurance and ACC

- Observed: ACC's Motor Vehicle levy is paid automatically through the fuel pump, currently 6 cents a litre, or through the annual vehicle licensing fee for non-petrol vehicles such as diesel or electric cars, and its no-fault cover for road-accident injury follows the vehicle rather than any registered operator or platform. (https://web.archive.org/web/20250811170444/https://www.acc.co.nz/about-us/our-levies-2/paying-levies-if-you-own-or-drive-a-vehicle)
- Inferred (assumption): because that cover is universal and compulsory, there is no separate "ACC relationship" a car-share brand holds that a new entrant would lack; ACC pays for personal injury only, not damage to the vehicle, which is the real gap on the insurance side.
- Observed: ICNZ's consumer-facing motor insurance page makes no mention of car sharing, peer-to-peer rental or rideshare, so the industry body has not published general guidance on the subject. (https://www.icnz.org.nz/individuals/motor/)
- Observed: MyCarYourRental's cover was underwritten by Ando and brokered by Crombie Lockwood, described by its founder as "the first cover of its type in New Zealand." (https://www.ajg.co.nz/news/breaking-new-ground-in-kiwi-rental-vehicle-market/)

## Earlier peer-to-peer platforms

- Observed: YourDrive, founded in July 2012 by Oscar Ellison, was New Zealand's first peer-to-peer car-sharing company; owners set their own price and the platform kept about 60% of the revenue. (https://futurefive.co.nz/story/nz-s-car-sharing-platform-ups-its-game-with-new-feature)
- Observed: rental car company Jucy owned 50% of YourDrive. (https://autotalk.co.nz/yourdrive-hopes-to-relaunch-after-paying-creditors-report/)
- Observed: more than 25,000 vehicle owners and renters had registered with YourDrive by June 2019. (https://futurefive.co.nz/story/nz-s-car-sharing-platform-ups-its-game-with-new-feature)
- Observed: YourDrive ceased operations from 10 March 2020, as its site put it, "given the current economic conditions," after the COVID-19 border closure removed its international tourist booking base. (https://autotalk.co.nz/yourdrive-hopes-to-relaunch-after-paying-creditors-report/)
- Observed: yourdrive.co.nz is now a domain-parking "for sale" page, confirmed by a direct fetch on 3 October 2026 and matching a Wayback Machine capture from 10 July 2026. (https://yourdrive.co.nz/)
- Observed: MyCarYourRental, founded in 2015, advertised "over 300 cars located nationwide" with full insurance cover on every rental, as captured by the Wayback Machine on 19 August 2025. (https://web.archive.org/web/20250819135021/https://www.mycaryourrental.com/)
- Observed: its site no longer loads, checked 3 October 2026. (https://www.mycaryourrental.com/)

## Ownership outside the profiled set (Ezi Car Rental, Toyota Financial Services)

- Observed: Toyota Finance New Zealand Limited, trading as Toyota Financial Services, completed its acquisition of Ezi Car Rental, a large Kiwi owned and operated rental car business, on 2 October 2023. (https://www.toyota.co.nz/about-toyota/toyota-news/2023/october/toyota-acquires-large-kiwi-owned-and-operated-rental-car-business/)
- Observed: Toyota's own announcement says Toyota Financial Services "will run Ezi as a separate business as it has with other acquisitions such as Cityhop," and that the deal "sits alongside our ownership of Cityhop, putting Toyota in a great position," so Cityhop and Ezi Car Rental share the same ultimate owner. (https://www.toyota.co.nz/about-toyota/toyota-news/2023/october/toyota-acquires-large-kiwi-owned-and-operated-rental-car-business/)

## Travel and tourism

- Observed: overseas visitor arrivals to New Zealand were 3.63 million in the year to March 2026, up 305,000 on the year to March 2025, including a rise of 161,000 to 1.56 million from Australia. (https://www.stats.govt.nz/information-releases/international-travel-march-2026/)
- Observed: domestic tourism expenditure was $27.5 billion against $16.9 billion of international tourism expenditure, in the official Tourism Satellite Account for the year ended March 2024. (https://www.tia.org.nz/the-industry/quick-facts-and-figures)

## Sport sponsorship

- Observed: New Zealand Rugby earns more than half the sponsorship and commercial revenue across New Zealand's roughly 100 national sporting organisations, and New Zealand Rugby, cricket and netball together account for more than three-quarters of it. (https://sportnz.org.nz/media/lgwh4ezn/3_funding-of-the-new-zealand-sport-play-and-active-recreation-sector-final-report.pdf)
- Observed: New Zealand Rugby's own FY2025 accounts reported sponsorship and licensing income of $142.214 million, up from $125.818 million in FY2024. (https://www.nzrugby.co.nz/assets/2025-NZR-Annual-Report_web.pdf)
- Observed: Super Rugby Pacific (the Blues, Chiefs, Hurricanes, Crusaders and Highlanders) and the National Provincial Championship are established rugby competitions distinct from the All Blacks themselves. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Provincial_Championship)
- Inferred (assumption): a paywalled article, seen only as a search snippet, put local sponsorship, merchandise and hospitality revenue for an NPC team at about $3 million, well below New Zealand Rugby's own $142 million sponsorship and licensing line; that is directionally consistent with a provincial deal costing a fraction of a national one, but the figure itself is not independently confirmed.
- Observed: Destination Queenstown's own site brands the town as "the adventure capital of the world" and lists skiing and snowboarding "at Coronet Peak or The Remarkables" among its winter activities. (https://www.queenstownnz.co.nz/things-to-do/adventure-activities/)
- Observed: the Queenstown Marathon's published event schedule lists race day as Saturday 14 November 2026, with marathon, half-marathon, 10km and kids' distances. (https://queenstown-marathon.co.nz/athlete-info/event-schedule/)

## EV running costs

- Observed: from 1 April 2024, electric and plug-in hybrid vehicles must hold a road user charges licence, at $76 per 1,000km for light EVs and $53 per 1,000km for PHEVs plus an admin fee, since PHEVs also pay fuel excise duty. (https://www.motortraders.govt.nz/about-us/news-and-notices/electric-vehicles-and-plug-in-hybrid-electric-vehicles-need-to-have-a-road-user-charges-licence/)
- Observed: the Clean Car Discount, the purchase subsidy that had helped drive EV uptake, ended on 31 December 2023. (https://vehicleinspection.nzta.govt.nz/news/clean-car-discount-to-end-31-december-2023)

## Vehicle fleet

- Observed: New Zealand had 4.7 million motor vehicles in 2024, up from 3.2 million in 2005, at 815 light vehicles per 1,000 people, high by international standards. (https://ehinz.ac.nz/assets/Surveillance-reports/Released_2025/NumberOfVehicles-Nov-2025.pdf)
- Inferred (assumption): New Zealand's rental car fleet is often put at about 26,700 vehicles holding 12-month licences as of March 2024, but that number traces back to Statista via IBISWorld, reprinted inside a rental operator's own promotional press release, not a primary government count; NZTA's own fleet-statistics pages block automated access, so no primary source could replace it. (https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/BU2602/S00147/new-zealand-car-rental-industry-expands-fleet-and-regional-services.htm)
