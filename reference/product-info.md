# Turo

## Overview

- Observed: Turo is a peer-to-peer car-sharing marketplace where private car owners ("hosts") list their own vehicles for guests to book by the day or longer, with Turo handling booking, payment processing, insurance and protection plans, and support. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turo_(company))
- Observed: founded in August 2009 in Boston as RelayRides, rebranded to Turo in November 2015. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turo_(company))
- Observed: headquartered in San Francisco. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turo_(company))
- Observed: operates in five countries: the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, France, and Australia. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turo_(company))
- Inferred (assumption): Turo has no current presence in New Zealand. No NZ operations turned up in company sources, NZ car-sharing coverage, or NZ market reporting; the only related mention found was a 2026 Facebook post from a NZ EV owners group asking whether there is a market for Turo there.

## Target Customers

- Guests: travelers and locals wanting a wider or cheaper vehicle selection than a traditional rental counter, including unique, exotic, or EV options.
- Hosts: private car owners monetizing an idle vehicle, ranging from casual single-car hosts to semi-professional multi-car "power hosts."
- Observed: 140,000 active hosts and 3.5 million active guests. (https://sacra.com/c/turo/)

## Problems We Solve

- Wider, more flexible vehicle selection than agency rental, without airport counters or fixed fleet limits.
- Turns a depreciating, mostly-idle private asset into an income source for its owner.
- Reduces peer-to-peer trust and liability friction through ID verification and built-in insurance/protection tiers.
- Fills gaps where agency rental supply is thin: sold-out weekends, remote areas, last-minute bookings.
- Observed: roughly 340,000 active vehicle listings across 16,000+ cities. (https://sacra.com/c/turo/)

## Core Product Areas

- Guest-facing booking marketplace and search.
- Host listing tools, including an earnings estimator ("Carculator").
- Tiered insurance and protection plans; the plan a host picks sets their earnings share.
- Trust and safety: ID verification, claims handling.
- Host payout system.
- Observed: from March 31, 2026, Turo applies a variable host share in nine US markets (Austin, Dallas, Detroit, Las Vegas, Maui, Philadelphia, Phoenix, San Diego, Seattle) that changes with the host's earnings plan and how far ahead a guest books. (https://turo.com/us/en/car-rental/united-states/host-hub/updates/2026-marketplace-updates)

## Pricing Model

- Observed: Turo's commission runs roughly 15-45% of the trip price depending on vehicle, duration, and market, averaging near 25%; the host keeps 70%, 80%, or 90% of the trip price depending on which protection plan they select. (https://sacra.com/c/turo/)
- Observed: guests pay the trip price plus a service fee and any protection or extras they add. (https://sacra.com/c/turo/)
- Inferred (assumption): the shift to demand-based variable host share in select US cities from March 2026 suggests Turo is actively testing dynamic, market-by-market commission rather than holding a single global rate.

- Observed: 2024 gross booking value reached $2.5 billion, of which $1.5 billion was paid out to hosts. (https://sacra.com/c/turo/)
- Observed: net income was $154.7 million in FY2022 and fell to $14.7 million in FY2023. (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1514587/000162828024036158/turoinc-sx1a11.htm)
- Observed: Turo has been EBITDA-positive for four consecutive years as of 2024. (https://sacra.com/c/turo/)
- Observed: Turo withdrew its IPO filing in early 2025 and cut approximately 15% of its workforce, around 150 employees, citing economic uncertainty. (https://techstartups.com/2025/04/16/car-rental-startup-turo-lays-off-15-of-workforce-after-halting-ipo-plans-amid-market-uncertainty/)
- Observed: Turo's own 2026 Road Report states US bookings for trips scheduled June through August 2026 were up 21% year over year. This is a Turo-published report, so treat the figure as a vendor claim about its own business, not an independently audited number. - Inferred (assumption): net income falling from $154.7M to $14.7M while revenue kept growing suggests Turo was already spending more to defend growth before the 2025 IPO withdrawal and layoffs, which matters for how much margin room it would have to subsidize a small new market like New Zealand rather than expecting it to be self-funding quickly.

## Primary Differentiator

- Asset-light marketplace model: no owned or leased fleet, so inventory is broader and more varied (including unique, exotic, and EV vehicles) than a traditional rental agency provides, at the cost of the consistency and guaranteed availability that an owned fleet gives.
