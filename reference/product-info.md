# Turo

*Re-checked: 2026-10-03*

## Overview

- Observed: Turo is a peer-to-peer car-sharing marketplace where private car owners ("hosts") list their own vehicles for guests to book by the day or longer, with Turo handling booking, payment processing, insurance and protection plans, and support. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turo_(company))
- Observed: incorporated as RelayRides, Inc. in Delaware in August 2009; publicly rebranded as Turo in November 2015, with the formal legal name change to Turo Inc. following in March 2016. (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1514587/000162828024036158/turoinc-sx1a11.htm; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turo_(company))
- Observed: headquartered in San Francisco (111 Sutter Street). (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1514587/000162828024036158/turoinc-sx1a11.htm)
- Observed: operates in five countries: the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, France, and Australia. (https://www.autorentalnews.com/news/turo-to-launch-vehicle-delivery-service)
- Inferred (assumption): Turo has no current presence in New Zealand. Its site navigation lists only the US, Australia, Canada (EN/FR), France, and UK, per an April 2026 Wayback capture; searches found no NZ job listings, NZ operations, or NZ company registration for Turo.
- Observed: in October 2022, when Turo confirmed its Australian launch, a spokesperson for Turo's Australian PR agency had nothing to announce for New Zealand, telling NZ trade outlet Autotalk: "We don't have anything to share at the moment, but Turo will continue to evaluate all opportunities..." (https://autotalk.co.nz/turo-car-share-service-confirmed-for-australia-not-for-nz/)
- Observed: People Inc. (formerly IAC Inc., renamed in June 2026) holds approximately 33% of Turo, per its 10-Q for the quarter ended June 30, 2026. (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1800227/000162828026051881/ppli-20260630.htm)
- Observed: Canada Basketball named Turo an official partner in March 2024, tied to the Olympic cycle. (https://www.basketball.ca/news/canada-basketball-announces-turo-as-an-official-partner)
- Inferred (assumption): this sponsorship does not appear to be active in 2026; Canada Basketball's July 2026 sponsor-renewal announcements (Rentals.ca, Canadian Tire Jumpstart) don't mention Turo.

## Target Customers

- Guests: travelers and locals wanting a wider or cheaper vehicle selection than a traditional rental counter, including unique, exotic, or EV options.
- Hosts: private car owners monetizing an idle vehicle, ranging from casual single-car hosts to semi-professional multi-car "power hosts."
- Observed: Turo defines an active host as one with at least one trip as a host in the trailing 12 months; under that definition it reported approximately 165,000 active hosts as of June 30, 2024. (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1514587/000162828024036158/turoinc-sx1a11.htm)
- Observed: at year-end 2024, Turo reported 3.5 million active guests and 140,000 active hosts. (https://web.archive.org/web/20251122122046/https://turo.com/blog/news/celebrating-13-years-of-drive/)

## Problems We Solve

- Wider, more flexible vehicle selection than agency rental, without airport counters or fixed fleet limits.
- Turns a depreciating, mostly-idle private asset into an income source for its owner.
- Reduces peer-to-peer trust and liability friction through ID verification and built-in insurance/protection tiers.
- Fills gaps where agency rental supply is thin: sold-out weekends, remote areas, last-minute bookings.
- Observed: at year-end 2024, Turo had roughly 340,000 active vehicles listed across 16,000+ cities in five countries. (https://web.archive.org/web/20251122122046/https://turo.com/blog/news/celebrating-13-years-of-drive/)

## Core Product Areas

- Guest-facing booking marketplace and search.
- Observed: host listing tools include an earnings estimator ("Carculator"). (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1514587/000162828024036158/turoinc-sx1a11.htm)
- Tiered insurance and protection plans; the plan a host picks sets their earnings share.
- Trust and safety: ID verification, claims handling.
- Host payout system.
- Observed: on March 31, 2026, Turo renamed "protection plans" to "earnings plans" across all markets; starting April 2, 2026, it began rolling out variable, demand-based host earnings in nine US markets (Austin, Dallas, Detroit, Las Vegas, Maui, Philadelphia, Phoenix, San Diego, Seattle), with more markets to follow later in the year. (https://web.archive.org/web/20260410033144/https://turo.com/blog/news/introducing-earnings-plans/)

## Pricing Model

- Observed: Turo's standard host share is 70%, 80%, or 90% of the trip price, depending on which protection/earnings plan the host selects. (https://web.archive.org/web/20260410033144/https://turo.com/blog/news/introducing-earnings-plans/)
- Inferred (assumption): Turo's blended commission is often cited as roughly 15-45% of the trip price, averaging near 25%, but this range traces only to an aggregator (Sacra) and could not be confirmed against Turo's own S-1/A, which describes guest service fees qualitatively but discloses no blended take rate.
- Observed: guests pay the trip price plus a service fee, charged as a percentage of the total booking value, and any protection plan or extras they add. (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1514587/000162828024036158/turoinc-sx1a11.htm)
- Inferred (assumption): the shift to demand-based variable host share in nine US markets from April 2026 suggests Turo is actively testing dynamic, market-by-market commission rather than holding a single global rate.

## Business Model & Unit Economics

- Observed: net revenue was $879.7 million in FY2023, up 18% from $746.6 million in FY2022. (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1514587/000162828024036158/turoinc-sx1a11.htm)
- Observed: net revenue reached $958 million in 2024, up about 9% from the $879.7 million in 2023. (https://web.archive.org/web/20251122122046/https://turo.com/blog/news/celebrating-13-years-of-drive/)
- Observed: gross booking value was $2.061 billion in FY2022 and $2.410 billion in FY2023. (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1514587/000162828024036158/turoinc-sx1a11.htm)
- Observed: 2024 gross booking value reached $2.5 billion, of which $1.5 billion was paid out to hosts; Turo says it has paid hosts more than $5 billion in cumulative earnings since its 2012 launch. (https://web.archive.org/web/20251122122046/https://turo.com/blog/news/celebrating-13-years-of-drive/)
- Observed: net income was $154.7 million in FY2022 and fell to $14.7 million in FY2023. (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1514587/000162828024036158/turoinc-sx1a11.htm)
- Observed: Turo has been Adjusted EBITDA-positive (a company-defined non-GAAP measure) for four consecutive years through 2024. No public figure exists for 2025 or 2026, since Turo withdrew its IPO filing and stopped disclosing financials. (https://web.archive.org/web/20251122122046/https://turo.com/blog/news/celebrating-13-years-of-drive/; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1514587/000162828024036158/turoinc-sx1a11.htm)
- Observed: Turo withdrew its IPO registration with the SEC on February 13, 2025, and in April 2025 cut about 150 jobs, roughly 15% of staff, citing "ongoing economic uncertainty." (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1514587/000162828025005472/turo-formrw.htm; https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-04-16/car-rental-startup-turo-cuts-15-of-workers-after-icing-ipo)
- Observed: Turo's own 2026 Road Report states US bookings for trips scheduled June through August 2026 were up 21% year over year. This is a Turo-published report, so treat the figure as a vendor claim about its own business, not an independently audited number. (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/turos-2026-road-report-growing-affordability-crisis-is-reshaping-how-americans-access-own-and-travel-by-car-302778285.html)
- Inferred (assumption): net income falling from $154.7M to $14.7M while revenue kept growing suggests Turo was already spending more to defend growth before the 2025 IPO withdrawal and layoffs, which matters for how much margin room it would have to subsidize a small new market like New Zealand rather than expecting it to be self-funding quickly.

## Primary Differentiator

- Asset-light marketplace model: no owned or leased fleet, so inventory is broader and more varied (including unique, exotic, and EV vehicles) than a traditional rental agency provides, at the cost of the consistency and guaranteed availability that an owned fleet gives.
