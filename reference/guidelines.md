# Competitor Profile Guidelines

Every competitor profile in `profiles/` must follow this template. Consistency enables cross-competitor analysis and automated research updates.

Framing lens for this analysis: New Zealand market entry for Turo - competitive tension, cultural fit for peer-to-peer car sharing, and how sports partnerships could build the brand there. Sections 2, 3, and 4 below are marked `(core)` because they carry that lens; the light pass (survey depth) populates those plus the Snapshot. Only the full-depth deep dive populates every section.

## Labeling Convention

All claims in profiles must be labeled:

- **`- Observed:`** - Factual claim with inline source URL
  - Example: `- Observed: Pricing starts at $99/mo (https://competitor.com/pricing)`
- **`- Inferred (assumption):`** - Speculation or interpretation without direct source
  - Example: `- Inferred (assumption): likely targeting enterprise based on demo-only sales motion`
- **Unlabeled bullets** are treated as inferred by default

This separation is critical - it lets research updates diff against known facts, and analysis reports flag confidence levels.

## Profile Sections

Every profile must include these sections in order:

### 1) Snapshot (required)

Who they are, what they do, who they serve. Include:
- Company name, founding year, headquarters
- Primary product/service description
- Target market segments and buyer personas
- Geographic focus
- Notable customers or case studies (if public)
- Recent funding, acquisitions, or major announcements

### 2) NZ Regulatory & Insurance Environment (core)

How they operate within, or would need to navigate, New Zealand's specific rules. Include:
- How insurance and liability are handled (their own policy, ACC interaction, or a partner insurer)
- Parking arrangements: dedicated spaces, council deals, or none
- Road-user charges or other EV-specific costs, if the fleet includes EVs
- Licensing or age restrictions on drivers/guests
- Any regulatory friction they've hit publicly (council policy, transport authority rulings)

### 3) Brand-Building & Local Marketing Channels (core)

How they build trust and awareness in New Zealand specifically. Include:
- Sponsorships, events, or partnerships used to build the brand (sporting, community, or otherwise)
- Primary acquisition channels (word of mouth, local press, paid, partnerships)
- How they talk about trust and safety in their own marketing
- Whether their marketing leans on a NZ-specific identity or a generic global one

### 4) Local Competitive Tension (core)

Where they actually stand against the rest of the field. Include:
- Market share or scale signals (fleet size, membership numbers, city coverage)
- Who they compete with most directly today, and on what basis (price, convenience, trust)
- Momentum: growing, static, or shrinking, with evidence
- Backing that changes their staying power (corporate parent, strategic investor)

### 5) Fleet, Coverage & EV Mix

What they actually offer. Include:
- Fleet size and vehicle types
- Cities or regions covered
- EV share of the fleet and any EV-specific positioning
- Booking model: free-floating, back-to-base, or a hybrid

### 6) Pricing & Packaging (required)

How they charge. Include:
- Public pricing (if available) with URL
- Pricing model (per-user, per-seat, usage-based, quote-only)
- Tier names and what's included at each level
- Free tier or trial availability
- Enterprise/custom pricing indicators

### 7) Differentiators vs Turo (required)

Direct comparison. Include:
- Where they are stronger than Turo
- Where they are weaker than Turo
- Where they are roughly on par
- Focus on dimensions that matter to Turo's buyers

### 8) Risks for Turo (required)

Threat scenarios. Include:
- Switching cost advantages they hold
- Market momentum or installed base threats
- Upcoming features or strategic moves that could impact Turo
- Partnerships or ecosystem advantages

### 9) Opportunities for Turo (required)

Favorable angles. Include:
- Gaps in their positioning Turo can exploit
- Customer pain points they don't address well
- Market segments they're ignoring
- Messaging or positioning weaknesses

### 10) Open Questions (required)

Things still unknown. Include:
- Data points to verify from further research
- Pricing details that aren't public
- Product capabilities that are unclear
- Strategic direction that's speculative

## Writing Style

- Use bullet points, not paragraphs
- Neutral, factual language - this is intelligence, not marketing
- Every Observed claim needs an inline URL in parentheses
- Keep profiles scannable - someone should get the picture in 2 minutes
