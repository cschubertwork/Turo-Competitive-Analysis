# Mevo

## Summary

*Category: Direct (NZ car-share) | Depth: full | Researched: 2026-09-22 | Re-checked: 2026-10-03*

| Dimension | Mevo | Turo |
|---|---|---|
| Model | Free-floating (Wellington) / hub model (Queenstown), hourly-to-daily pricing | Peer-to-peer marketplace, host-owned cars |
| Geography | Wellington, Queenstown (relaunch); lost Auckland, Hamilton, Nelson from its 4-city peak, Queenstown is new | US, Canada, UK, France, Australia |
| Status | Relaunched after March 2026 collapse, operating in Wellington and Queenstown | Operating, EBITDA-positive |

### 1) Snapshot

- Observed: Mevo was founded in Wellington in 2014 by Erik Zydervelt and Finn Lawrence, launching in late 2016 with Audi A3 e-tron plug-in hybrids. (https://autotalk.co.nz/mevo-enters-voluntary-administration-weeks-after-3-28m-crowdfunding-raise/)
- Observed: at its peak, Mevo operated across four cities with a mixed fleet including Teslas, Polestars, and Suzuki Swifts. (https://autotalk.co.nz/mevo-enters-voluntary-administration-weeks-after-3-28m-crowdfunding-raise/)
- Observed: Mevo expanded into Hamilton in 2021 by acquiring Loop from Ebbett Group, entering a NZ$30 million vehicle supply agreement at the same time. (https://autotalk.co.nz/mevo-enters-voluntary-administration-weeks-after-3-28m-crowdfunding-raise/)
- Observed: Mevo Limited and Mevo Assets were placed into voluntary administration on 30 March 2026, weeks after raising NZ$3.28 million through equity crowdfunding platform Snowball Effect, a raise that had exceeded its NZ$2.2 million target. (https://autotalk.co.nz/mevo-enters-voluntary-administration-weeks-after-3-28m-crowdfunding-raise/)
- Observed: an investor was set to give Mevo NZ$1.7 million that would have seen it through administration, but pulled out because Mevo breached some conditions of the deal. (https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/business/591275/mevo-car-sharing-service-goes-into-voluntary-administration)
- Observed: Nelson lost its car-sharing option after Mevo's exit; the city council reallocated the nine car parks across three central city carparks that had been set aside solely for Mevo back to general public use. A councillor who helped enable the original arrangement called the reversal a sign of how fragile such initiatives can be. (https://business.scoop.co.nz/2026/06/10/nelson-car-sharing-stalls-after-mevo-exit/)
- Observed: Carbn was founded in July 2020. (https://nzbusiness.co.nz/technology/acquisition-targets-low-carbon-fleet-transition)
- Observed: Carbn Group Holdings acquired the brand and assets of collapsed car-sharing operator Mevo in May 2026, after the two companies had been in a dispute with each other. The deal bought Mevo's assets out of liquidation rather than the insolvent entity's shares; Mevo owed creditors NZ$4.3 million at the time. The relaunched business now trades as Carshare NZ Limited, part of the Carbn Group. (https://autotalk.co.nz/carbn-group-acquires-mevo-to-relaunch-wellington-car-sharing/; https://businessdesk.co.nz/article/finance/from-dispute-to-deal-mevo-acquired-by-car-share-rival-carbn; https://mevo.co.nz/faq)
- Observed: the Mevo acquisition aligned with Carbn Group's existing Zilch network of 20 shared-vehicle electric hubs at the time of the May 2026 deal, meaning Carbn already owned Zilch before acquiring Mevo; in September 2026 Carbn's CEO said Zilch was expanding to 31 hubs nationally, including five airports. (https://autotalk.co.nz/carbn-group-acquires-mevo-to-relaunch-wellington-car-sharing/; https://www.thepost.co.nz/business/361084229/mevo-returns-wellington-streets-after-eight-months-limbo)
- Observed: the Wellington relaunch features a fleet of Suzuki Swifts operating under the Mevo brand, with plans for national expansion and a broader range of electric vehicle types as the business develops. (https://autotalk.co.nz/carbn-group-acquires-mevo-to-relaunch-wellington-car-sharing/)
- Observed: Mevo currently operates in Wellington and Queenstown only. (https://mevo.co.nz/)
- Inferred (assumption): Mevo's pre-collapse fleet mix (Teslas, Polestars) was likely too capital-intensive to run profitably at its scale; the relaunch's shift to cheaper Suzuki Swifts reads as a direct correction to that.
- Inferred (assumption): raising more than its crowdfunding target and still collapsing within weeks suggests the failure was a short-term cash-flow and timing problem (the specific fallen-through NZ$1.7M commitment) rather than an inability to attract capital in general.

### 2) NZ Regulatory & Insurance Environment

- Observed: pre-collapse, one Mevo support article set the standard insurance excess at NZ$4,000 per trip, reducible to NZ$1,000 (Enhanced Cover) or NZ$0 (Total Cover) for an hourly surcharge, while a separate Mevo support article gave different figures for the same named tiers (NZ$2,500 Standard, NZ$500 Enhanced, NZ$0 Total); neither article states which figures were current or when the change happened. (https://web.archive.org/web/20260401090702/https://support.mevo.co.nz/en/articles/6419293-mevo-insurance-covers)
- Observed: the relaunched Mevo's FAQ describes insurance, alongside parking and road-user charges, as bundled into the hourly or daily rate; no selectable excess tiers are published on the current site. (https://mevo.co.nz/faq)
- Observed: Mevo negotiated a cost-neutral "Home Zone" parking arrangement with Wellington City Council in May 2018, letting its vehicles use council parking spaces beyond its original 10 designated areas. (https://ecommercenews.co.nz/story/mevo-teams-wellington-city-council-be-free-floating-car-share)
- Observed: this arrangement did not carry over automatically to the relaunch; Carbn Group spent roughly eight months waiting for Wellington City Council to re-approve the 18 on-street car parks previously allocated to Mevo before the brand could fully return. (https://www.thepost.co.nz/business/361084229/mevo-returns-wellington-streets-after-eight-months-limbo)

### 3) Brand-Building & Local Marketing Channels

- Observed: Mevo's Automobile Association (AA) partnership offers AA members up to NZ$100 in free Mevo credit in their first year, covering Auckland, Wellington, and Hamilton. (https://autotalk.co.nz/car-share-operator-mevo-joins-forces-with-the-automobile-association/)
- Observed: Mevo's fleet was owned and maintained by Mevo and powered by Meridian Energy. (https://www.meridianenergy.co.nz/ev/mevo)
- Observed: Mevo's pre-collapse backers included Z Energy, which built an equity stake of up to 32% over time, along with Ebbett Group and Icehouse Ventures; Mevo also received more than NZ$1 million in EECA grants. Meridian Energy, Mevo's charging and energy-supply partner, never held equity. (https://autotalk.co.nz/mevo-enters-voluntary-administration-weeks-after-3-28m-crowdfunding-raise/)
- Inferred (assumption): no sports sponsorship or event partnership found for Mevo, pre-collapse or relaunched, across its own site (current and archived), AA/Meridian/Z Energy partnership coverage, and general searches for Mevo sports or event sponsorship in New Zealand. Its brand-building has run through motoring-body and energy-company partnerships (AA, Meridian, Z Energy equity) rather than sport or culture.

### 4) Local Competitive Tension

- Observed: pre-collapse, Mevo was described in press coverage as "Wellington car-sharing king" when Auckland Transport policy changes opened the door to its Auckland launch. (https://www.nzherald.co.nz/business/companies/freight-logistics/new-at-policy-sees-wellington-car-sharing-king-mevo-hit-go-for-auckland-launch/KN5VOZHI5AJQBZVKQUEKWMOIZ4/)
- Observed: Carbn Group Holdings, Mevo's owner, is majority owned by Mobility Holdings Limited (91.42%), with New Zealand Private Capital Management Limited (4.81%) and Carbn Share Trustee Limited (3.77%) holding the rest; its registered office is 181 Montreal Street, Christchurch. New Zealand Green Investment Finance, the Crown's green investment bank, was a shareholder until Carbn bought out its stake. (https://app.companiesoffice.govt.nz/companies/app/ui/pages/companies/8042826/shareholdings; https://autotalk.co.nz/carbn-buys-out-govt-owned-nzgif-stake-targets-low-emissions-fleet-growth/)
- Observed: fleet management provider Custom Fleet has reported that Carbn Group is now its subsidiary, though this was not yet reflected on the NZ Companies Office register as of 3 October 2026. (https://autotalk.co.nz/custom-fleet-strengthens-ties-with-mobility-technology-firm-carbn/)
- Inferred (assumption): Mevo's collapse despite being called the Wellington market leader, and despite an over-subscribed funding round, is the single most important data point in this whole analysis for reading Turo's own margin discipline against a New Zealand launch. Local market leadership did not translate into a business that could survive one funding delay.

### 5) Fleet, Coverage & EV Mix

- Observed: the Wellington relaunch fleet operates under the Mevo brand as Suzuki Swifts; Carbn Group bought about 40 of Mevo's red-branded Suzuki vehicles when it acquired the brand, relaunching with 12 cars on 22 September 2026 and planning to scale to an initial 38. (https://autotalk.co.nz/carbn-group-acquires-mevo-to-relaunch-wellington-car-sharing/; https://www.thepost.co.nz/business/361084229/mevo-returns-wellington-streets-after-eight-months-limbo)
- Observed: pre-collapse, Mevo ran fifteen plug-in hybrid Audi A3 e-trons around Wellington CBD and Wellington International Airport. (https://thespinoff.co.nz/business/14-03-2018/the-primer-the-car-sharing-service-putting-electric-vehicles-on-wellingtons-roads)
- Observed: New Zealand Herald coverage of Mevo's Auckland expansion describes the Auckland Transport policy change as authorising a "free-floating" car-share model for Mevo, meaning vehicles can be picked up and left within a zone rather than returned to a fixed base. (https://www.nzherald.co.nz/business/companies/freight-logistics/new-at-policy-sees-wellington-car-sharing-king-mevo-hit-go-for-auckland-launch/KN5VOZHI5AJQBZVKQUEKWMOIZ4/)
- Observed: Wellington's current Mevo operation is free-floating, adding cars and pickup locations as the fleet grows, while Queenstown runs a hub model, picking up from hotels and set locations around town rather than anywhere in a zone. (https://mevo.co.nz/cities)

### 6) Pricing & Packaging

- Observed: current Wellington pricing runs three tiers: Compact at NZ$19/hour or NZ$95/day, Comfort at NZ$22/hour or NZ$110/day, and Premium at NZ$25/hour or NZ$125/day. (https://mevo.co.nz/wellington/pricing)
- Observed: each of Mevo's three Wellington pricing tiers lists the first 50km per trip as free, with additional distance charged at NZ$0.39/km; the trip-cost calculator further down the same pricing page states a 100km free allowance instead, a live discrepancy on Mevo's own site that was still unresolved as of 3 October 2026. (https://mevo.co.nz/wellington/pricing)
- Observed: charging, parking, insurance, and road-user charges are all included in the price. (https://mevo.co.nz/wellington/pricing; https://mevo.co.nz/faq)
- Observed: there is no fixed membership or joining fee mentioned in Mevo's pricing pages; the model is pay-as-you-go by the hour or day. (https://mevo.co.nz/faq)

### 7) Differentiators vs Turo

- Inferred (assumption): Mevo is stronger than Turo on immediacy and short-trip convenience in Wellington specifically: free-floating pickup anywhere in its zone beats Turo's model of booking a specific host's car in advance, for a same-day or spontaneous single trip. Queenstown runs a hub model, so this comparison does not extend to that city.
- Inferred (assumption): Turo is stronger than Mevo on selection and trip length: Turo's marketplace of individually owned cars spans far more vehicle types and easily supports multi-day trips, while Mevo's relaunched Wellington fleet is a single, deliberately cheap vehicle type (Suzuki Swift). Queenstown's vehicle type was not confirmed in any source found.
- Inferred (assumption): Turo is structurally stronger on capital risk: it owns no fleet at all, so it carries none of the vehicle-financing exposure that appears to have driven Mevo's collapse. Any NZ entry Turo made would inherit this same asset-light advantage over Mevo's model.
- Inferred (assumption): pre-collapse Mevo offered selectable in-app insurance tiers comparable to Turo's; the relaunched Mevo's public materials describe insurance as bundled into the price with no selectable tiers, so post-relaunch Mevo may now be behind Turo on this specific trust-tool rather than on par. Not confirmed without testing the current app's checkout flow.

### 8) Risks for Turo

- Inferred (assumption): Mevo's brand damage from a public collapse could make New Zealand consumers more skeptical of any new vehicle-access startup, including a Turo entry, at least in the near term after March 2026. Mevo's own relaunch has drawn mainstream "comeback" coverage, which could cut the other way over time.
- Inferred (assumption): Mevo's AA partnership (not confirmed as still active post-relaunch) and its Wellington City Council parking history are a smaller moat than a simple incumbent advantage: Carbn itself spent roughly eight months renegotiating council approval for Mevo's on-street parks before relaunching, so a new entrant's path to a council parking deal looks similar to the one the incumbent just walked.
- Inferred (assumption): this risk is weaker than it first appears. Z Energy built an equity stake of up to 32% in pre-collapse Mevo, and Meridian Energy was its charging and energy-supply partner rather than a shareholder. Carbn's 2026 deal bought Mevo's brand and assets out of liquidation rather than the insolvent entity's shares, so no pre-collapse investor stake, Z Energy's included, carries into the relaunched business Turo would be competing with.

### 9) Opportunities for Turo

- Inferred (assumption): Mevo's retreat from Auckland, Hamilton, and Nelson leaves those specific cities without the free-floating car-share option they briefly had, which is a gap Turo's marketplace model (no fleet to place city by city) could fill without the capital risk that sank Mevo.
- Inferred (assumption): Mevo's collapse is a concrete, recent, local cautionary tale that Turo could use in its own market-entry narrative or investor conversations to differentiate its asset-light model from fleet-owning NZ operators, though Mevo's September 2026 relaunch means the story is now one of collapse and comeback rather than a closed case.
- Inferred (assumption): no NZ car-share operator found in this research, Mevo included, uses sports sponsorship for brand-building. That is open ground for a new entrant, not a channel Turo would need to fight an incumbent for.
